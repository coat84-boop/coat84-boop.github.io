Professional Reflections

My Journey, Education, and Professional Goals

I have had a unique career path. When I joined the Army in 2007, my experience was limited to the trades. I worked as a general contractor, a siding and roofing contractor, and even as a UPS truck loader. When I joined the Army, I became a truck driver. After about 5 years of driving trucks for the Army, I decided to try and use my brain instead of my body. I reclassed to 25S, or Satellite Communication Systems Operator and Maintainer. I spent the next 8 years of my life learning everything from satellite telemetry and spectrum data, to configuring a router and switch, to rebuilding an entire Active Directory Domain. I also earned the rank of SFC, and attended and graduated from the Advanced Leaders Course and Senior Leader Course, providing essential leadership skills to effectively lead in a multitude of operational environments. Now that I am no longer serving, I'm enjoying my time as the Director of Digital Workplace and Technology services for the Hospital Industry Data Institute in Jefferson City, MO.

While serving and working, I also attended Western Governors University for roughly 7 total years. I earned a Bachelor of Science in Network Operations and Security in 2020, a Master of Science in Cybersecurity and Information Assurance in 2022, and an MBA in IT management in 2024. I'm currently enrolled at the University of Missouri - Columbia in their IS_LT Learning Technologies and Design Graduate program with an anticipated graduation of Spring 2028. While earning these degrees, I also earned the following industry certifications: CompTIA A+. CompTIA Network+, CompTIA Project+, CompTIA Cloud Essentials, CCNA, LPI Linux Essentials, and CEH. Combined with my real world experience, I have a unique blend of technical skills and communication skills that make me an asset to nearly any organization due to my ability to communicate complex technical concepts into business needs.

While I'm currently serving in director-level role, I would eventually like to move my way into a CISO/CIO position. I have a strong background in cybersecurity, a unique grasp of risk management frameworks, and the curiosity and tenacity to keep looking, and find things others don't. I believe my ability to communicate, which I have cultivated over a lifetime of being misunderstood, is my biggest strength. It helps bridge gaps, lower defensive posture, and provides a safe environment for others to thrive, and be held accountable for their own actions. I have excellent time management skills, and rarely require work outside of business hours. If I can't get it done from 9-5, I'm doing something wrong.

Ultimately, I would like to work as hard as I can for another 13 years and retire at 55. I already have excellent income, I own a home, and I'm not in crippling debt. Over the next decade and change, my wife and I will deliberately build a life centered around long-term comfort. A smaller home that's more sustainable. More property for our animals to enjoy and more privacy for us. And between my VA disability, my CRSC, the pension I'll receive from my time here at HIDI, and my wife's VA disability, we should have a nice comfortable life.

I have added some "unique" traits to my web page, and my CSS that make my website respond in very specific ways. Some of these traits are only on certain pages, I will eventually merge all pages with like functionality so the feel of the site is consistent. One thing I noticed is that when you click on the "Download My Resume" button on my About Me page, it opened the PDF right there in that window. I didn't like that functionality, so I red further and learned how to change my code to make it open up in a new tab altogether. By adding "target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"" to my a href="webcontent/resume.pdf" class="resume-link" syntax. The target="_blank" tells the browser to open up a new window when viewing the resume, and the rel="noopener noreferrer" adds security and privacy inherent with the site your on to the pdf you're opening. I liked this setup a lot more, as the viewer doesn't have to move away from the resume to review the rest of the site, it's just there and you can swap between tabs.
