WHERE IT ALL BEGAN

My Professional Journey

From the Trades to Technology

I have followed an unconventional career path. Before joining the United States Army in 2007, my experience was primarily in the trades. I worked as a general contractor, a siding and roofing contractor, and even as a UPS truck loader.

When I enlisted, I became a truck driver. After approximately five years in Army transportation, I decided to pursue a career that would challenge me intellectually rather than physically.

I reclassified to 25S, Satellite Communication Systems Operator-Maintainer, and spent the next eight years developing technical skills ranging from satellite telemetry and spectrum analysis to router and switch configuration and Active Directory administration.

During my military career, I also earned the rank of Sergeant First Class and graduated from both the Advanced Leader Course and Senior Leader Course. Those experiences strengthened my ability to lead teams in demanding operational environments.

Today, I serve as the Director of Digital Workspace and Technology Services for the Hospital Industry Data Institute in Jefferson City, Missouri.

PEOPLE BEFORE TECHNOLOGY

My Leadership Philosophy

Communication Is My Greatest Strength

Although I currently serve in a director-level role, I remain interested in advancing into executive technology leadership, potentially as a Chief Information Security Officer or Chief Information Officer.

My background in cybersecurity, understanding of risk management, and curiosity have shaped the way I approach complex problems. I believe in looking beyond the obvious and continually asking how systems and processes can be improved.

More than anything, I consider communication my greatest strength. Having spent much of my life learning how to make myself understood, I recognize the importance of creating environments where people feel comfortable asking questions, sharing ideas, and accepting accountability.

I also value effective time management and sustainable working habits. I believe successful leadership means building systems and teams that can perform effectively without relying on constant work outside normal business hours.

Great leadership isn't just about understanding technology. It's about understanding people.

LOOKING TOWARD THE FUTURE

Personal & Professional Aspirations

Building a Meaningful Future

My long-term goal is to continue working, learning, and contributing over the next several years, with the hope of retiring around age 55.

Along the way, I want to continue developing as a leader, expanding my education, and pursuing opportunities that allow me to make a meaningful difference.

Beyond my professional ambitions, my wife and I hope to build a quieter and more sustainable lifestyle, with a smaller home, more land, room for our animals, and greater privacy.

Ultimately, success means more than professional recognition or financial achievement. It means creating a life that reflects our priorities and gives us the freedom to enjoy what matters most.
